## Overall Impression

The homepage makes an unusually specific and credible argument. The CSS mockups are doing narrative work that most B2B landing pages outsource to stock photography. The hero question earns its hook. The HVAC before/after case study is the most specific claim of its kind on any comparable vendor site.

The single biggest opportunity: the page is set up like a story but delivers like a catalog in the second half. After the Shift section peaks emotionally, the page defaults to frameworks, grids, and feature lists. The conversion architecture is also confused — the Free AI Audit is the primary conversion action, but it is buried as the fifth card in a service grid alongside paid offerings. Everything before it earns the click; the moment of the ask is where the page loses focus.

## What's Working

**1. The CSS mockups are doing serious narrative work.**
AiRecommendMockup, HvacAiMockup, and MetricsDashboardMockup are not decorative. Each is an argument. The #32→#2 HVAC before/after is unusually specific and therefore more credible than a screenshot of a generic analytics dashboard. Most landing pages use stock photos as section ornament; these mockups make the company's specific claim visible.

**2. The hero question is earned.**
"When customers ask AI, does it recommend you?" creates a knowledge gap that the page then closes over several sections. Most hero headlines state a benefit. This one tests the visitor first — appropriate for franchise operators who think they already have their digital presence handled.

**3. The animation system is production-quality and purpose-appropriate.**
GSAP implementation with reduced-motion detection, mobile IntersectionObserver fallback, section-specific clip-path reveals, and scroll-trigger staggered word reveals. Each section has a specific animation rationale; mobile degrades gracefully. `initMobile()` using IntersectionObserver is particularly clean.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] LLM Link Strip breaks conversion momentum at the worst possible moment

**What**: The "Ask an AI platform about us" strip sits between the Services section and the closing CTA. It invites users to open ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Gemini in a new tab immediately before the page's only closing conversion opportunity.

**Why it matters**: Even if the user returns, conversion intent is interrupted at exactly the moment the page has spent ten sections building. Worse: if the AI platforms return weak or inconsistent answers about Shark AI Solutions, the entire page premise collapses in the tab that was just opened. This is either the most powerful proof-of-product on the site or its most dangerous moment — and the page treats it as a low-stakes afterthought.

**Fix**: Move the strip to immediately after the "As Seen In" press logos section, where skepticism is highest and the concept of "we're already recommended by AI" needs validation. At that position it is proof; at its current position it is distraction.

**Suggested command**: `$impeccable layout src/app/page.tsx`

---

### [P1] Reviews section has no loading state or fallback

**What**: `<review-widget>` is a third-party web component loaded via `<Script strategy="lazyOnload">`. The CDN URL contains a double-dot (`client..prod`) that may be a typo. If the script fails, the section renders only the H2 "Real businesses. Real reviews." over blank space.

**Why it matters**: For a page whose entire narrative is built on trust signals, an empty Reviews section actively destroys credibility — precisely at the moment the page is trying to deliver social proof.

**Fix**: Embed 2–3 static testimonials as fallback content inside the section; show the widget only when loaded. Also verify the CDN URL (`cdn.apigateway.co/review-widget-client..prod/sdk.js`) is correct.

**Suggested command**: `$impeccable harden src/app/page.tsx`

---

### [P1] Free AI Audit treated as peer to paid service offerings

**What**: The Free AI Audit is the fifth card in a 5-item service grid, rendered at the same visual weight and size as "AI Business Consulting" and "AI Employees." A free, zero-commitment action is categorically different from a paid engagement.

**Why it matters**: The audit is the primary conversion micro-step — it is the thing the hero CTA, the closing CTA, and the page's entire architecture is pointing toward. Burying it in a grid reduces its perceived value and signals nothing about its distinctiveness. A franchise decision-maker scanning the grid sees five options with no hierarchy. The audit gets lost.

**Fix**: Pull the Free Audit out of the grid. Give it its own callout row with a badge or differentiated visual treatment ("No cost. No sales call. Results in 24 hours."). The remaining four services can stay in a 2×2 grid.

**Suggested command**: `$impeccable layout src/app/page.tsx`

---

### [P2] "Found. Trusted. Chosen." cards are the emotional dead zone of the page

**What**: After the Shift section peaks ("Search has changed. Most businesses haven't." + vivid stats + the AI recommendation mockup), the Framework section lands on three identical parchment cards with checklist items. It reads like a scope-of-work document.

**Why it matters**: This is the first emotional valley on the page, and it arrives immediately after the strongest section. Users are primed for a payoff and receive a framework explainer instead.

**Fix**: Introduce one real-world outcome per column (specific client vertical, specific result), or visually differentiate the three states (Found = neutral, Trusted = building, Chosen = highlighted/active) to make the framework feel like a journey rather than a taxonomy.

**Suggested command**: `$impeccable bolder src/app/page.tsx`

---

### [P2] Team section is a missed credibility inflection point

**What**: The two paragraphs in the Team section are the most direct "why us" content on the page, followed by a text link to `/about`. No team photos, no named individuals, no inline credibility element. The EmployeeCarousel component is imported in the file but never rendered on this page.

**Why it matters**: At the moment a franchise operator is considering trusting this company with their multi-location reputation, the page offers two paragraphs of assertion and an exit link. The import of `EmployeeCarousel` at line 5 suggests this was considered and removed — but the section still needs a proof element of some kind.

**Fix**: Add 2–3 named team quotes with headshots or a simplified team credential strip (name + title + one sentence). Alternatively, if EmployeeCarousel is now unused on this page, remove the import to avoid dead code.

**Suggested command**: `$impeccable delight src/app/page.tsx`

---

### [P3] Secondary hero CTA duplicates the scroll

**What**: The "See How It Works" button in the hero navigates to a section already visible by scrolling — no anchor link, just a page-level reference. There is no reason for a button of this visual weight to direct users to scroll.

**Why it matters**: Two equal-weight CTAs in the hero create a choice where there should be a single clear action. The secondary CTA diffuses the conversion intent without adding meaningful navigation value.

**Fix**: Reduce the secondary CTA to a text link (`↓ See how it works`), or replace it with something distinctly different in kind from the primary CTA (e.g., "Watch a 60-second demo" if that asset exists).

**Suggested command**: `$impeccable clarify src/app/page.tsx`

## Minor Observations

- `EmployeeCarousel` is imported at line 5 but never rendered in the homepage JSX. The static 5-card grid in the Employees section replaces it. Remove the import if the component is not used on this page.
- The hero image (`/ai-visibility-hero.webp`) has no `placeholder` or `blurDataURL` prop, meaning the LCP candidate loads with no skeleton. The `aspectRatio: '16/9'` container collapses if the image fails.
- Typography utility classes (`.display-hero`, `.display-lg`, `.display-md`) are defined in `globals.css` but every headline in `page.tsx` uses raw `style={{ fontSize: 'clamp(...)' }}` instead. Type system changes require hunting individual `clamp()` values across the file.
- The `<Script>` CDN URL (`cdn.apigateway.co/review-widget-client..prod/sdk.js`) contains a double-dot that should be verified as intentional. A broken URL here silently fails (strategy="lazyOnload", no error boundary).
- The 5-item Services grid and 5-card AI Employees grid both exceed the cognitive load chunking rule (≤4 items per group) on the same scroll — two simultaneous violations amplifying each other.

## Questions to Consider

**1. If you removed every mockup from this page, what evidence remains that this company delivers results?**
The stat cards claim "$1M+ weekend revenue" and "#4→#1 Realtor" but with no client name, screenshot, or third-party verification. The mockups are CSS simulations. A skeptical franchise decision-maker needs proof objects — a named client, a screenshotted result, a media mention tied to a specific outcome — not simulated dashboards. What is stopping you from using real screenshots?

**2. Is the Free AI Audit the right first conversion step for a franchise operator?**
A 15-location franchise operator evaluating vendors is not going to book an audit before having a conversation. The current CTA architecture optimizes for the SMB owner who needs a quick hook. If the primary target is franchise/enterprise, should the primary CTA be a scoped conversation ("Request a franchise assessment") with the audit as the secondary, self-serve track for SMBs?

**3. What happens when your own AI ranking is inconsistent on the platforms you claim to help clients dominate?**
The LLM Strip invites users to ask ChatGPT and Perplexity about Shark AI Solutions in real time. If those answers are weak, partial, or show competitors ranked above you, the entire page premise fails in the browser tab the user just opened. Is this strip being monitored? Is the company's own AI visibility maintained as actively as client work?
